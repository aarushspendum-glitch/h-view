## Filming notes
- **The 3:00/2:00 split is deliberate**: judges need to actually see the product working (montage + demo), not just hear about it, but they're scoring you on 100 points of pitch content — so the talking segments have to be tight and every line has to earn its rubric tag. Nothing here is filler.
- **Entry lines matter more than they look** — "Cue the montage" and "Let's look live" are short, but they're what makes the prerecorded clips feel like part of the pitch instead of a cutaway. Don't cut them to save time.
- **Fill in bracketed placeholders** in Scene 2 with real numbers if you have them; cut the bracket if you don't — the line still works without it.
- **Don't cut the V³ words** in Scenes 12 and 14 ("our vision," "visibility strategy," and the explicit "vision, visibility, value" callback).
- If you're running slightly over 5:00 in the edit, trim from the montage/demo b-roll pacing (fewer redundant cuts) before cutting any talking-point line — the talking points are where the rubric points live.

## If you get asked a tough question (portal Q&A, follow-up email, or live round)
The one gap this script can't fully paper over: your pilots are free, so revenue/willingness-to-pay is not yet proven. If asked "how do you know people will pay once it's not free?" — the honest, strongest answer is: "We're deliberately sequencing trust before revenue. Free pilots let us prove reliability with zero risk to the business owner, which is exactly what makes the eventual subscription an easy yes instead of a cold sell." Say it plainly rather than getting caught off guard.
